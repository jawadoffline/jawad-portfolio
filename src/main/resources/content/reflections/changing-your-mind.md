Changing your mind is not a weakness.

We live in a time when everyone seems to have an opinion about everything. And once we have expressed that opinion, we somehow feel obligated to defend it, even when we begin to realize that we might be wrong. You see it in everyday conversations. Someone makes a statement, another person disagrees, and within minutes, what started as a simple discussion turns into an argument. Both sides become more interested in proving their point than understanding what the other person is trying to say.

And it happens everywhere. In families, among friends, at workplaces, and especially on social media. The strange part is that sometimes we know the other person has a valid point. We just don't want to admit it.

Perhaps because we have started treating being wrong as a personal failure. As if accepting someone else's argument somehow makes us less intelligent or less capable. But nobody gets everything right.

We form opinions based on what we know, what we have experienced, and what we have been taught. Over time, we learn new things, meet different people, and begin to see situations from perspectives we hadn't considered before. There is nothing embarrassing about changing your mind when you have learned something new.

Of course, this doesn't mean agreeing with everyone or abandoning your principles just to avoid an argument. There are things worth standing firm on, and there will always be disagreements that cannot be resolved. But there is a difference between having strong beliefs and being unwilling to question them.

Sometimes the most sensible thing you can say in a conversation is, You know what, I hadn't thought about it that way.

It doesn't make you weak. It doesn't take anything away from you. And not every disagreement needs to end with someone winning and someone losing.

Sometimes a conversation serves its purpose simply because you walk away understanding something you didn't understand before.
