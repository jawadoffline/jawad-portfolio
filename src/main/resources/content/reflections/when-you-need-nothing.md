The way you treat people when you need nothing from them says a lot about you.

It's interesting how differently some people behave depending on who they are dealing with. They are polite to someone who can offer them a job, an opportunity, a favour, or a useful connection. They are patient with people who have influence and careful with those who might be important to their future. But that same patience and respect can disappear when they are dealing with someone who has nothing to offer them.

You see it in offices, restaurants, shops, and even within families. Someone speaks respectfully to a senior manager but talks down to the office assistant. Someone is charming with a business client but unnecessarily rude to the waiter serving their table. And quite often, people don't even realize they are doing it.

There is nothing wrong with building connections or treating professional relationships seriously. We all need connections, and naturally, some relationships will demand more of our time and attention. But basic respect should not depend on someone's position, financial situation, or usefulness to us. A person doesn't become more deserving of courtesy because they have an impressive job title. And someone doing an ordinary job doesn't become less deserving of it.

The same applies when relationships change. A colleague who can no longer help your career, a friend going through financial difficulties, or someone whose circumstances have changed should not suddenly become less important as a person. Of course, nobody can give equal time and attention to everyone. We have our own responsibilities, boundaries, and priorities.

But how we speak to people, how we acknowledge them, and whether we treat them with basic dignity are choices we make every day.

It is easy to be respectful when there is something to gain. The more revealing question is how we behave when there isn't.

Respect should be part of our character, not a strategy for getting ahead.
